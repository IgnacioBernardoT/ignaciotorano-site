Famous Tampa Homes · No. 7 · 1925

The D.P. Davis House on Davis Islands

The Mediterranean Revival home of the developer who created Davis Islands during Florida’s 1920s land boom.

Home › Famous Tampa Homes › D.P. Davis House
Address116 W. Davis Boulevard, Tampa
Built1925
StyleMediterranean Revival
NeighborhoodDavis Islands
Known ForD.P. Davis

Davis Islands exists because developer David Paul “D.P.” Davis imagined turning dredged land near downtown Tampa into a Mediterranean-style residential community. In 1925, while the project was still being built, Davis began construction on his own house at 116 W. Davis Boulevard.

The Man Behind Davis Islands

Davis became one of the best-known developers of Florida's 1920s land boom. His Tampa project transformed mudflats and small islands near the mouth of the Hillsborough River into a planned neighborhood marketed around waterfront living, Mediterranean Revival architecture and easy access to downtown.

A House Designed for the Developer

The National Register record dates the house to 1925 and identifies Franklin O. Adams Jr. as its architect. The design uses the same Mediterranean Revival vocabulary seen throughout the original Davis Islands development: stucco walls, clay tile, arched openings, decorative ironwork and an asymmetrical composition.

A Brief Time in His Own Creation

Davis did not have long to enjoy the house. In October 1926, while traveling aboard the ocean liner Majestic, he went overboard and died at age 41. His death came as Florida's land boom was collapsing, but the neighborhood that bears his name endured.

The house was listed on the National Register of Historic Places in 1989 as part of the Mediterranean Revival Style Buildings of Davis Islands multiple-property listing.

Did You Know?

The man who created Davis Islands actually built his own residence inside the development — making this house one of the most direct links between a Tampa neighborhood and the person whose name it carries.

"Davis Islands is one of those places where the original development plan is still easy to read in the streets, architecture and village center almost a century later." — Ignacio Toraño
